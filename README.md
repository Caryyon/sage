# SAGE: Self-Adaptive General Explorer

SAGE is an experimental artificial general intelligence (AGI) system that learns to create complex patterns entirely on its own - and more importantly, learns *how to learn*. Think of it as a digital organism made of thousands of tiny cells that work together, similar to how biological cells form living tissue. Each cell observes its neighbors and decides how to change, creating self-organizing patterns that actively maintain themselves like living systems.

What makes SAGE different from traditional AI is its **13 cognitive abilities** that enable autonomy and self-awareness. It monitors its own learning and adjusts its strategy automatically (meta-learning). It generates hypotheses and explores novel ideas autonomously (curiosity engine). It spawns lightweight test agents to try out theories in parallel (agent manager). It notices when it's struggling, tracks which skills transfer between tasks, and even grows or shrinks its neural network based on problem complexity. These abilities work together to create a system that doesn't just follow instructions - it actively manages its own learning process.

The system trains through a 4-phase curriculum where complexity builds progressively. It starts with 4 parallel agents learning basic terrain types (mountains, hills, plains, valleys), progresses through geometry, then advances to terrain generation where emergent civilizations spontaneously appear - villages in valleys, mining towns in mountains, fishing ports near water, and trade hubs in strategic locations. Finally, its curiosity engine takes over and autonomously discovers novel pattern combinations never seen in training. You can watch all of this unfold in real-time through four visualization modes: parallel multi-agent training, civilization emergence on learned terrains, survival testing (can patterns self-regulate?), and a dashboard showing all 13 cognitive features in action.

SAGE represents a step toward general intelligence by demonstrating that when you combine self-modification, introspection, curiosity, and autonomous decision-making, you get something more flexible and adaptive than narrow AI. It's an exploration of what makes intelligence "general" rather than specialized - systems that can learn across domains, transfer knowledge, reflect on their own processes, and make autonomous decisions about what to explore next.
